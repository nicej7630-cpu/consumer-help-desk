Scam alerts

Scams change often, but the warning signs are usually the same. If something feels urgent, pressured or too good to be true, stop and check before you act.

Never give out: your banking password, a one-time code, your card's security number, or your PIN. A genuine bank or business will never ask for these.

Fake delivery messages

A text asks you to pay a small fee, often a few pounds, to receive a parcel.

  • Don't click the link in the message.
  • Check the tracking number directly on the courier's own website or app.

Too-good-to-be-true offers

Very cheap goods, luxury items at tiny prices, or unknown sellers with no reviews.

  • Search the seller's name with the word "scam" or "reviews".
  • Pay by card or a method that offers buyer protection, not by bank transfer to a stranger.

Urgent bank or payment requests

A call, text or email says your account is locked, or a payment is about to be taken, and asks you to act now.

  • Hang up, and call your bank on the number printed on your card or on its official website.
  • Never share a one-time code, even if the caller seems to know your details.

Investment and romance scams

Someone online promises high returns, or builds a relationship and then asks for money.

  • Be wary of anyone who asks you to send money quickly, or to a new account.
  • Speak to family or a friend before sending any money.
Think you've been scammed? Contact your bank straight away, and report it to the police or to the consumer authority for your country. The sooner you act, the more can be recovered.
